GMARTBrand identity proposal · Three directions · 24 November 2026

Three ways to brand Rwanda's new supply network.

GMART will import from Egypt and sell to Kigali's cafés, bakeries, mini-markets, hotels and homes, first over WhatsApp and later through its own web app. Each direction answers the same brief in its own voice, with a logo, colour, type, tone of voice and the first things we would print.

  1. 01 · STORY-LED
    Upstream

    The route from the Nile's source and back. Calm, warm, a little premium.

  2. 02 · SYSTEM-LED
    Manifest

    The brand looks like the supply chain it runs. Clear, coded, built for B2B trust.

  3. 03 · PEOPLE-LED

    A pattern for every category. Bold, joyful, made to win shoppers and feeds.

The brief

What the business plan asks of a brand

  • PLAN · P.2B2B will drive volume. B2C will drive brand visibility, reach and additional sales.

    The identity must earn a purchasing manager's trust and still stop a thumb on TikTok.

  • PLAN · P.7We will test products through WhatsApp before investing in technology.

    The first brand surface is a small round profile photo and a catalogue card. The mark has to hold up there.

  • PLAN · P.4Packaging is potentially one of GMART's most attractive B2B categories.

    GMART will sell tape, labels and kraft boxes. Its own brand should look right printed on them, in one colour.

  • PLAN · P.32I wouldn't claim that GMART is the cheapest.

    The advantage is convenience, relationships and data. The brand should sound clear and dependable, never discount-loud.

  • PLAN · P.16We should begin identifying suppliers in other countries if Egypt cannot provide competitive prices.

    Egypt is the starting point, not the whole story. The idea has to survive a second sourcing country.

  • PLAN · P.33December 1–10: Publish GMART branding.

    Two weeks. Every direction here can launch with stickers, tape, a WhatsApp kit and a social grid.

The design problem

One name has to work for a hotel buyer ordering 2,000 cups and for a student buying one box of Egyptian wafers.

Direction 01Story-led

Upstream

Closer to the source.

The idea

One of the Nile's farthest headwaters rises in Nyungwe Forest, in south-west Rwanda, and runs about 6,650 km north to the Mediterranean. GMART works the same route in reverse, carrying Egyptian goods back upstream to Kigali.

Geography

Rwanda is where the Nile begins. GMART is the return trip, and every shipment retells it.

Trade

In supply chains, upstream means closer to the maker. Buying closer to the maker is the whole reason wholesale costs less.

Language

In Kinyarwanda, isoko names a market and also a spring, the place water rises. The idea is already in the language.

Confirm with native speakers

The metaphor outlives Egypt. If GMART later sources from Turkey or Kenya, the promise stays the same: we go to the source so your business doesn't have to.

The idea

GMART looks like the supply chain it runs. Every surface borrows from the paperwork of trade: the waybill, the carton stencil, the thermal label, the customs stamp. To a café owner it signals order and reliability, and it turns the plan's most disciplined habit, calculating every landed cost, into the brand itself.

Show the math

Every product card shows what sits inside the price: product, freight, duties, clearance. Businesses restock with numbers they can check.

Everything has a code

GM-PKG-014. Products, shipments and customers each get one. Reordering becomes a short WhatsApp message.

One colour, any surface

Built for thermal printers, rubber stamps and kraft cartons. Launch costs stay small and the look never breaks.

Direction 03People-led

Good Patterns

Stock up on good.

The idea

Rwanda and Egypt both have deep geometric traditions: the zigzag panels of Imigongo from eastern Rwanda, and the star-and-lattice tilework of old Cairo. Good Patterns gives GMART a pattern for every category, so a box of wafers, a sleeve of cups and a bag of flour each say where they came from, from across a shop.

Four categories, four patterns

Grocery chevrons, café dots, packaging stripes, supplies diamonds. Shelves, the app and invoices colour-code themselves.

The logo is the catalogue

Four tiles, one per category from the plan. When GMART opens a new category, the mark gains a tile.

Made with Rwandan artists

We would commission the pattern library with Imigongo artisans rather than imitate it, and credit them on the packaging.

Recommendation

Lead with Upstream, and run its operations on Manifest's code system.

Upstream is the only direction with a story no one else in Kigali can tell. It gives Vyral a route, a set of makers and a place to film for three years, and it still works when a second sourcing country arrives. Its weak spot is the warehouse floor, where Manifest is strongest. So we borrow Manifest's product codes, thermal labels and price-breakdown cards, set them in Upstream's Nile and Gold, and keep one brand.

Choose Manifest instead if the first year is almost entirely B2B and the budget for print is tight. Choose Good Patterns if the plan shifts toward retail and gifting, where it would outsell both.

Before anything ships
  • Isoko. Confirm with native Kinyarwanda speakers that the word carries both meanings, market and spring, the way Upstream uses it.
  • The Nile claim. Nyungwe is one of several places named as the Nile's farthest source. Keep the wording "one of" in all copy.
  • Patterns. Good Patterns should be co-created and credited with Imigongo artisans, not drawn from reference images.
  • Numbers on mockups. Prices, percentages, codes and phone numbers shown here are placeholders and illustrations, not quotes.
